How it works

What the percentage on each problem means, where it comes from, and how well it has held up on attempts it never saw.

The number

Every recommended problem carries one number: the chance that your first submission to it is accepted. The five shown are the ones nearest 50% — hard enough to teach you something, and still problems people at that level solve in the end about nine times in ten.

Where it comes from

A model learned from 4,000 other Codeforces users rated 1000–1999 and their 1.58 million first attempts. About each problem it knows:

  • how often people get it right first time — some problems are traps, whatever their rating says;
  • how hard its topics are for everybody;
  • where it sits in its contest.

About you, it uses your rating, how your own past attempts went — recent ones counting more — and what you have practised in the last hour, day, week and month. For a new account's first six rated contests Codeforces computes with a higher rating than the profile shows, and the model uses that one. Somebody with no rating yet starts from {{ unrated_start }}, the value that predicted best for people who had not entered a rated contest, and their own practice moves them from there.

Your visit does not train it. The model is learned from that separate sample and refitted once a month; your history is only used to place you inside it.

How well it holds up

It was tested on the {{ ev.attempts }} first attempts made in 2026, none of which it learned from. The score is log loss: the average penalty per prediction, where a confident wrong answer costs far more than a cautious one. Lower is better. Guessing 50% every time scores {{ "%.3f"|format(ev.coin) }}; guessing the usual success rate every time, {{ "%.3f"|format(ev.average) }}.

What it cannot know

  • Whether you read the editorial first, how long you spent, or whether it was a bad day. Even the true probability leaves one attempt a coin toss.
  • Which topics you are weak at. Measured, a user's strength in a topic added almost nothing: people choose their own problems, and a weakness hides in the choosing. So the results page does not claim it.

The details

Every number here, how it was measured, and the ideas that did not work are in the project's specification and decision records. The 2026 attempts were scored twice, once for the first version of this model and once for this one, each time with the configuration committed before the test — why that matters.
